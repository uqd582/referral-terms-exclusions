## What Counts as a "Qualified Referral"

This is the part most people skim and then regret. A referral is only "qualified" when all three of the following are completed within 180 days of your friend's signup date:

- **Step 1 — Signup via your unique link or code.** Your friend must use your specific referral link or enter your referral code during registration. They also must sign up on a unique device (mobile, desktop, or tablet) — sharing a device with you can forfeit the bonus.
- **Step 2 — Accumulate $10 or more in valid spend through ShopBack.** This is spend, not cashback. The friend needs to make purchases totaling at least $10 through ShopBack links. Orders that don't earn cashback, or that are canceled, exchanged, or refunded, do not count toward the $10 minimum.
- **Step 3 — Add withdrawal details.** Your friend has to add their own withdrawal information (e.g., PayPal account) to their ShopBack account.

If all three aren't done within 180 days, the referral flips from "Pending" to "Rejected" and nobody gets paid. There's no partial credit.

ShopBack is fairly aggressive about policing abuse. The terms list the following as prohibited and grounds for losing your bonuses (and, for repeat offenses, your entire account):

- **Self-referral.** You cannot refer yourself, even with a second email or device.
- **Posting your referral link on ShopBack merchant Facebook pages or forums.** So no dropping your link in a brand's official community.
- **Disguising your referral link as a generic link** and embedding it in web pages to trick people into clicking.
- **Keyword bidding** to drive traffic to pages containing your referral link. If you're running paid ads on referral-related keywords, that's a no.
- **"Any similar activity"** that ShopBack, in its sole discretion, decides is inconsistent with the program's purpose. This is a catch-all — they reserve the right to make judgment calls.

The practical takeaway: share your link openly with people you actually know, in channels where you're not impersonating a brand or buying attention. Personal messages, your own social accounts, your own blog or newsletter — all fine. Spamming merchant pages or running ads on the keyword — not fine.
